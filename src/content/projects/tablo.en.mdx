Tablo draws and checks the diagrams of Belgian electrical installations, from
the survey on site to the file handed to the inspection. It is made for the
residential and commercial installer — the person who, for every house sold or
renovated, has to produce a single-line diagram and a position plan that pass
the approved inspection body.

Today that work is done in CAD suites built for panel builders — powerful,
expensive, slow to learn —, in free tools that draw but understand nothing, or
on paper and in Excel. Tablo takes the other side: little to learn, and
software that knows what it is drawing. It runs in the browser and as a desktop
app (Linux, macOS, Windows) from the same code, and it is in free beta at
tablo-app.be.

## On site, on a phone

Everything starts with the survey, and the survey is done standing up, in the
house, with a phone. Room by room: pick the type of room and the floor, and add
the sockets, lights, switches and appliances in one tap each, with their
quantity. Every point belongs to a circuit, picked or created on the spot with
the usual values — a 30 mA RCD is offered by default for a bathroom or a
washing machine.

The board is surveyed the same way: main switch, RCDs, breakers, cables and
lengths, and the measured resistance of the earth electrode. The survey says
what is still missing while you are still there, which is the only time that
is any use.

There is no signal in a cellar. The app installs from the site and works fully
offline: projects and photos stay on the device, and a cloud project opens from
its local copy and syncs when the connection comes back.

## The board, in a photo

Copying an existing board out by hand is the most tedious part of the job. You
photograph it instead: the AI reads the devices — ratings, curves, RCDs,
labels — and proposes the list of circuits, which the installer checks before
it goes into the project. Circuits already surveyed keep their points.

The same way in takes a sentence, typed or dictated — "kitchen, circuit C, six
sockets on 2.5 mm², 20 A" —, a hand sketch, or a request to the assistant that
edits the project: "add an EV charger, then generate the multi-line diagram".
Whatever the AI proposes goes through a review step, and nothing changes the
project except through the same undoable operations the editor uses. The
server can also run a model hosted on your own machine instead of Claude, with
the same features and the same checks.

## One model, two drawings

The heart of Tablo is that it does not draw two diagrams: it keeps one model
and shows it twice. The single-line diagram, per board, and the position plan,
per floor, are generated from the survey and tagged the same way — A1, A2,
B1… as on the board —, so a point on the plan is always a point on a circuit.
When the survey changes, the diagrams are regenerated in place, and a rule
flags any that have fallen behind.

The same model gives the multi-line diagram, the control schematics, the board
layout on its rails, wire and terminal numbering, and cross-references between
sheets. The architect's plan can sit underneath: an image, a PDF, or a DXF file
placed at true scale with no calibration.

## The checks, while you draw

Because the software knows what a circuit is, it can check one. The RGIE rules
run as you work: 30 mA RCDs in wet rooms, points per circuit, cable sections
and ratings, voltage drop, maximum protected length. The calculations behind
them are the trade's own — design current, current-carrying capacity by
installation method, voltage drop, breaking capacity against the short-circuit
current at the origin.

Every finding explains the rule and says how to fix it. And Tablo also says
what it is not: an aid to preparing the inspection, not the inspection. That
stays with the approved body.

## What comes out

The file: sheets to size, with the RGIE title block filled from the project's
details, as PDF, SVG, PNG or DXF; the circuit list; the board's labels; a
handover pack with a QR code. And what comes right after the diagram in an
installer's actual working life: the bill of materials and the quote —
devices, cables by the diagram's own lengths, labour, prices from their
wholesaler's list, VAT at 21 % or 6 %, as an Excel file with its formulas.

## As a team

With an account, projects sync between phone, tablet and computer, live
between several people, with team workspaces and a shared library of symbols
and templates. A read-only link shows the project to the client or to the
inspection body, as it is or frozen at a revision. The app speaks French, Dutch
and English, with Flemish terminology on the Dutch side, and the language of
the drawings is a project setting, separate from the interface's. The server is
in Europe.

## What there is, and what there is not

It is a free beta, online and as a desktop app; the iPhone and Android versions
are waiting for their store listings. Belgium first, and the RGIE with it;
other countries' rules are in preparation and marked as such.
