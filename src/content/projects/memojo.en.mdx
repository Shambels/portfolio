## The problem

You have eleven thousand photos and no way to ask for one.

The search box in a gallery app matches filenames and dates, which is to say it
matches nothing you remember. What you remember is "the one of her laughing in
the garden", and the only tools that answer that sentence are the ones that
upload your entire life to somebody's datacentre first, then sell you the
storage back a year later when the free tier runs out. The privacy cost and the
lock-in are the same cost paid twice.

Memojo answers the sentence on the phone. Photos are read, understood and
grouped where they already are: search is natural language, albums build
themselves, nothing is tagged by hand, and there is no account, no upload and
no network call — the app works in aeroplane mode because there is nothing for
it to reach. Sharing an album is peer to peer and cross-platform, Android to
iPhone directly, with no size cap and no copy landing on a third machine on the
way.

It is on both stores, free, and it collects nothing.

## Where this one stands

This panel is shorter than the three beside it, and that is deliberate rather
than unfinished-and-quiet: the others are written up because their interesting
decisions are settled. Memojo's are not written down yet — what the on-device
model gives up against a server-side one, what the peer-to-peer transport does
on two phones that are not on the same network, and what "no byte leaves your
phone" costs in a feature list. That write-up belongs here and it is not here.

Until it is, the stores have the product, and the two links above go to it.
